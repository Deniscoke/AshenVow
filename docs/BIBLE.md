# ASHEN VOW — Story & World Bible (canonical)

> *What the ash remembers.*

This document is the single source of truth for story, characters, places, and **every shared identifier**
(maps, spawns, flags, items, NPC ids, sprites). Builders must use the IDs exactly as written here.
Region-local IDs you invent must be prefixed (see §12).

---

## 1. Premise in one breath

Nineteen years ago the kingdom of **Lanthorn** was dying of **the Greying**, a sickness that greys the skin and
eats memory until the sufferer forgets their own name and crumbles into walking ash. The sick were walled into the
poor lower district of the royal city, the **Wickward**. On the holy night of the Last Vigil, the Warden of the
Seventh Gate, **Ser Emeric Brand**, sealed the Wickward and set it alight — **the Kindling**. Thousands burned
with no names spoken over them. The fire never went out. It became the **Unending Pyre**, and its ash has fallen
on Lanthorn ever since, carrying the unnamed dead into every field, forest and grave.

Brand burned with them. He did not stay dead.

He wakes on a stone bier in a village crypt while a young Chandler tries to perform the death-rite over him —
and fails, because she does not know his name.

**Central question:** *Can a person truly redeem himself if the consequences of what he did can never be undone?*
The game never answers this for the player. The endings each answer it differently, and none is framed as correct.

**Tone:** melancholic, mysterious, lonely, beautiful, sometimes frightening, never nihilistic. People lived here.
Humor exists (dry, tired, human). Silence is used. Nobody says "Greetings, traveler."

## 2. The faith of the Wick (mythology)

- Every soul is a **flame** lit by the **Candlemother** on the great Wick of the World.
- When someone dies, a **Chandler** (priest; also literally candle-maker) performs **the Snuffing**: speaks the dead
  person's full name, then pinches out a candle. The spoken name lets the flame "go home". Chandlers say
  *"Go home, [name]. The dark is shorter than it looks."*
- A flame whose name is never spoken **cannot leave**. It lingers, gutters, forgets. The lost become:
  - **the Waning** — ghosts who have forgotten their names; often gentle, repeating fragments of their lives.
  - **the Unlit** — hollow burning husks; hostile; hungry for warmth.
- The faith's sign is the **Open Hand**: a cupped hand with a flame above it. Painted on a door it meant
  *"the Greying is within"* (quarantine mark). Players see it on a village door (Posy's house) early; its meaning
  becomes clear in the Wickward, where every door bears it.
- Oaths: *"By the Wick."* Mild curse: *"Mother's wick."* Farewell: *"Keep your flame."*
- **The Snuffer** is the Candlemother's patient servant who snuffs flames that overstay. Most people think it is a
  children's story.

## 3. Recurring symbols (plant these early, pay them off late)

1. **Wooden birds.** Crude little carved birds sit on nameless graves all over the village and beyond.
   Early: Mound says "Sister makes them. For the ones with no names. So they have something."
   Linnet, if asked: "My da carved them for me. He was bad at it. Every bird looked like a turnip with opinions."
   Late (Wickward infirmary): on an empty child's cot lies the *first* such bird — carved by Brand for his daughter.
   **Payoff: Linnet is Wren. The birds on every grave are Brand's own gesture, carried by his daughter.**
2. **Tin tags.** The quarantined sick wore stamped tin tags with their name, row and bed ("so their names would not
   be lost when they themselves forgot"). Brand wakes with a scorched tag tied to his wrist: `W · · EN`. He believes
   it belonged to his dead daughter. It did — but she did not die.
3. **The Open Hand** (quarantine mark), see §2.
4. **Grey hands.** Linnet's hands are grey to the wrist (arrested Greying — the abbey's Cold Rite). Players see this in
   her portrait from minute one. Abbey records later explain what grey hands mean.
5. **Tallow candles.** Brand heals with tallow candles that Linnet pours for him. His daughter has been keeping him
   alive the whole game.

## 4. Canon timeline

- **~300 years ago** — Saint Wendel, a wandering chandler, founds the pilgrims' Rest south of the royal city.
- **26 years ago** — War with the neighboring kingdom of **Carrow**. **Battle of Greyharrow Field**: Emeric Brand
  (24, a chandler's son from the Wickward) and his sworn brother **Odo Varr** hold the Harrow line for a night and a day.
  Brand is knighted — "the Shield of Greyharrow" — and later named **Warden of the Seventh Gate** of Hallowhearth,
  the gate that faces south to the Rest and overlooks the Wickward.
- Brand marries **Ilsabet** (weaver); she dies of fever when their daughter **Wren** is three. He raises Wren himself
  and carves her wooden birds, badly.
- **20 years ago** — the Greying appears in the Wickward. The Chandlers issue tin tags. The Wickward is walled.
- **19 years ago, autumn** — Wren (8) shows the grey. By law she is taken into the Wickward (Row 3, Cot 11).
  Brand visits her at the gate every evening.
- Prior **Anselm** of **Lenten Mere Abbey** discovers the **Cold Rite**: long immersion in the abbey's cold springs
  halts the Greying (the grey stays in the hands; the mind returns). Nurse **Agna** smuggles a few Wickward children
  out through the drains, **Wren among them**; Agna cuts Wren's tag off and leaves it on the cot so the count holds.
- Anselm writes to the Warden begging him to delay any burning and send the sick to the abbey; the letter mentions
  "a child of your own house, who improves daily." The courier is a young Chandler's runner named **Fenwick Moll**.
- **The council.** King **Aldemar** (weak), Queen **Ysolde** (wants to evacuate the sick to the abbey and the
  countryside), Archchandler **Ormund** (calls fire "the Candlemother's own mercy"), and Brand. A grey case is found
  inside the palace. **Brand proposes the Kindling.** "Every day we wait, the grey climbs the wall." He volunteers to
  light it. Ormund blesses it, the King signs, the Queen weeps. Afterwards everyone calls it "the King's order".
- **Fenwick delivers Anselm's letter.** Brand sees the abbey seal, knows it will beg for delay, and **burns it
  unread**. (Brand's SECRET. He has never told anyone. Fenwick saw.)
- **The Kindling**, the night of the Last Vigil. Brand seals the Wickward. Odo refuses the order and tries to open the
  gate; Brand strikes him down. Watchman **Hesk** sees everything from the wall. The Chandlers pour oil. **Brand lights
  it with his own torch.** Then he hears (or thinks he hears) a child — and goes *in*, through the fire, to Row 3.
  Cot 11 is empty, her tag on the pillow. He believes she is already ash. Burning, he closes his fist on the tag and
  swears: **"I will not lie down until every one of them is laid to rest."** — the **Ashen Vow**. The fire takes him.
- The fire never goes out: the **Unending Pyre**. The Greying ends — no one has shown the grey since that night.
  The ashfall begins, and Lanthorn dies anyway within a year. *(Did the fire work? It ended the plague. It ended the
  kingdom. The Cold Rite might have saved many, or only a few. No one will ever know.)*
- The Queen orders the Seventh Gate sealed with the three **Wardstones** of the three orders so the Pyre stays
  contained: the **Hart** (royal foresters, Hushwood), the **Bell** (Lenten Mere Abbey), the **Shield** (knights of
  Greyharrow). Each keeper carries their stone home.
- On Ormund's order, Fenwick opens the sluices of the Lenten Mere dam to drown the "heresy" of the Cold Rite. **Abbot
  Hollin** rings the great bell to warn the abbey and drowns in the belfry still ringing. Agna leads the children out
  of the high dormitory. Anselm drowns at his desk.
- Wren, renamed **Linnet** as a novice, grows up with the surviving sisters. At eighteen she becomes Chandler of
  Saint Wendel's Rest, tending the dead who drift in with the ash. Most have no names, so she cannot snuff them.
  She puts a wooden bird on each nameless grave. She believes her father died in the Kindling *trying to stop it*
  (the story the sisters told her). She has never connected "Brand who lit it" with "Da".
- **Now (Day 1)** — after a great ash-storm Linnet finds a burned knight's armor half-buried on the royal road, drags
  it to her crypt with the other dead, and tries the Snuffing. She doesn't know his name. The rite fails. He sits up.

## 5. The protagonist — Ser Emeric Brand

- **Silhouette:** tall knight; **wick-helm** (closed great-helm topped with a thin blackened spike like a candle wick —
  the Warden's helm); a single vertical visor slit glowing ember-orange; torn grey tabard bearing the Open Hand;
  heavy notched greatsword named **Vigil**; one pauldron cracked open with light inside; **ash constantly sifting from
  every seam**; a scorched tin tag knotted to his left wrist.
- **Curse, visible:** cracks glow like coals; ash leaks while he walks; when he Kindles, the cracks blaze and the ash
  becomes sparks. As he hollows, the slit-glow shifts from ember to a pale, cold white.
- **Voice:** terse, formal remnants of a knight; dry; long pauses (…); self-lacerating; occasional flashes of the warm
  man he was (he was funny once — Odo remembers). Dialogue choices let the player shade him: hard, honest, evasive,
  or kind. He is never a silent hero.
- **Guilt:** he lit it. He knows he lit it. He remembers that much from the first minute.
- **His SECRET:** (1) the Kindling was *his* proposal, not the King's order; (2) he burned Anselm's letter unread.
  Revealed piece by piece (Odo hints → Fenwick exposes the letter → the Queen confirms the proposal → Ormund
  gloats). If the player chooses, Brand can confess either piece earlier.
- **His FALSE BELIEFS:**
  1. *"The curse was laid on me as punishment."* — False. **He swore it.** The Ashen Vow bound him. The Snuffer tells
     him in the Wickward: "Nobody cursed you, Warden. You *promised*. I was there. I am always there."
  2. *"My daughter burned because of me."* — False. **Wren lived** — because of the monks whose letter he burned.
     Linnet is Wren. This is grace he did not earn, and it also means the Cold Rite worked, at least for some —
     which makes the Kindling look worse. Both are true.
  3. (Minor) *"I lit it and walked away."* — False. Hesk saw the knight go *into* the fire screaming a name.
     This complicates his self-image without excusing anything.
- **Personal history** surfaces through choices: chandler's son from the Wickward (he burned his own birthplace),
  Greyharrow hero, widower, a father who carved turnip-shaped birds.

## 6. Mechanics that carry story (engine implements; content references)

- **Death:** he cannot die. On death the armor collapses into ash, the screen reads **NOT YET**, and he re-forms at the
  last shrine. `deaths` +1, `hollow` +1. Enemies respawn. NPCs occasionally notice (Linnet: "You were a pile of ash on
  my floor this morning. I swept you into a corner and you got up and *walked out*.")
- **Hollow** (hidden, never shown as a number): +1 per death, +1 per Kindle, +3 per devoured flame, other cruelty.
  Mercy lowers it (see §11). Stages: 0–7 (stage 0), 8–15 (stage 1), 16+ (stage 2). Stage changes Brand's portrait,
  visor-glow colour, ash emission, and some dialogue. At stage 2 a few tender dialogue options appear greyed and
  unselectable ("[He cannot find the words.]").
- **Cinder / Kindle (the curse as power):** after Boss 1, a Cinder gauge fills as Brand kills and is struck (the ash
  of the slain streams into his armor). When full, **Kindle** (Q): ~8 s of blazing strength (double damage, fire trail,
  faster, stamina-free). Every Kindle is literally burning the dead inside him again: hollow +1, and faint whispered
  text drifts up ("…not again…", "…it's warm…", "…Da?…"). Players who look closely will realize what they are fuel.
- **Boss aftermath choice** (Hart, Bell, Odo): a fallen keeper's flame lingers. Options:
  **Speak its name** (only if the name was learned; hollow −2; small relic) — **Devour it** (Brand draws it into his
  armor; permanent +1 sword damage; hollow +3) — **Leave it** / region-specific option.
- **Tallow** (heal): Linnet's candles; charges refill at shrines. "It won't fix you. It'll make the dark shorter."
- **Eye-pennies** (currency): coins the dead wore on their eyes. Dropped by enemies. Spent at Fenwick.
- **Names:** 12 tin tags hidden in the world + Fenwick's Wickward Ledger. Spoken at the Pyre.

## 7. Regions & maps (contract)

World graph: the village **Saint Wendel's Rest** is the hub. Four roads: north → royal road → Seventh Gate →
Hallowhearth; east → Hushwood; west → Lenten Mere; south → Greyharrow. East/west/south are **barred** until Boss 1
(the village gate key from Linnet). North is open, but the Seventh Gate needs all three Wardstones.

Each map declares named **spawns**. Exits target `map:spawn`. The table below is the contract; each region builder
must create every listed spawn and every listed exit.

## 9. Bosses

1. **THE UNNAMED PROCESSION** (`boss_procession`, chapel) — pilgrims fused into one lurching mass bearing a burning
   reliquary palanquin, chanting "…Brand… Brand…" (the ash remembers him). Slams, pole sweeps, spawns pilgrims,
   charges the nave; phase 2 the reliquary cracks and rains cinders. Death: dissolves; its ash streams INTO Brand's
   armor. Linnet: "It went into you. The ash. It went *into* you." Sets `boss_procession`, `cinder_unlocked`.
2. **THE OLD HART, AUBRENN** (`boss_hart`, hart_barrow) — the Hushwood's ancient grave-warden stag, hollowed by ash;
   candle-antlers, bark-skin. Charges, antler sweeps, root lines, stomp shockwave; phase 2 antlers ignite. Holds the
   `hart_stone`. Its name "Aubrenn" is learnable from the forester's journal or the Moth-Wife.
3. **HOLLIN, WHO RANG THE FLOOD** (`boss_hollin`, abbey_belfry) — the drowned abbot, bloated, a great bronze bell
   grown into his ribs, still ringing to warn a dead abbey. Toll shockwave rings (dodge through them), water tendrils,
   bell slam; phase 2 the bell cracks: erratic double tolls. Holds `bell_stone`. Name known from the abbey.
4. **SER ODO VARR, THE OSSUARY WARDEN** (`boss_odo`, ossuary_heart) — Brand's sworn brother, fused into his burned
   plate, shield welded to his arm. Mirror of the player: combos, shield bash, leaping slam; phase 2 he tears off his
   helm and calls his company (if the roll-call was not completed, ghost soldiers join). Talks during the fight.
   Holds `shield_stone`. He calls Brand "Em" — the only one who does.
5. **WHAT THE ASH REMEMBERS** (`boss_final`, pyre) — the Pyre wears the shape the dead remember: phase 1 **the
   Kindler**, a burning knight with Brand's silhouette and a torch-sword, fighting as Brand fought; phase 2 it collapses
   and rises as **the Thousand**, a towering mass of ash faces and reaching hands around the fire. It does not die: it
   kneels and waits for his choice.

## 15. Voice guide (dialogue)

- Short lines. Subtext. People interrupt themselves. Ellipses are pauses, not decoration.
- Nobody explains the world to someone who should already know it. Brand *does* know the world; he lacks only
  pieces of one night. So lore arrives sideways: what people assume he knows, what they avoid saying.
- Each character must be identifiable with the name hidden.
- No modern slang. No "Greetings, traveler." No "Hark". No villain monologues.
- It is allowed for a scene to end on silence ("…") or on a character simply leaving.
- British-ish spelling is fine (colour, grey) — use **grey** consistently.
